# 05 — Quantum-Like and Classical Regimes of Relational Dynamics

This document formally constrains the meaning of **“quantum-like”** and **“classical”** regimes within the RES=RAG framework. These terms describe **relational behavior classes**, not claims about underlying physical particles or quantum fields.

This file exists to prevent:
- Literal misinterpretation of physical quantum mechanics
- Metaphorical drift
- Category collapse between physics and relational systems

---

## 1. Regime Language Is Structural, Not Ontological

Within RES=RAG:

- **Classical regime** = determinate, same-reference-frame relational dynamics  
- **Quantum-like regime** = indeterminate, cross-reference-frame relational dynamics

These terms refer to:
- **Behavioral structure**
- **Inference ambiguity**
- **Context sensitivity**
- **Collapse under observation**

They do **not** refer to:
- Planck constants
- Wavefunctions as physical entities
- Quantum tunneling
- Physical superposition of particles

---

## 2. Classical Regime (Same-Reference-Frame Dynamics)

### 2.1 Formal Definition

A relational system is in a **classical regime** when:

- All coupled distributions share a **single reference frame**
- Transport dynamics commute under observation
- Measurement does not alter the underlying state

This applies to:

- Axis I (Human–Human internal equilibrium)
- Axis III (Machine–Machine internal equilibrium)

---

### 2.2 Structural Properties of the Classical Regime

In the classical regime:

- Outcomes are:
  - Determinate
  - Repeatable under identical conditions
  - Observer-independent
- Transport dynamics:
  - Are path-dependent but reference-stable
  - Admit single explanatory decomposition

---

### 2.3 Classical Collapse

Collapse in the classical regime corresponds to:

- Standard failure modes:
  - Burnout
  - Mania
  - Hallucination
  - Sterility
- Collapse resolves ambiguity through:
  - Constraint violation
  - Over- or under-transport

There is **no observer-dependent interpretive ambiguity** in these collapses.

---

## 3. Quantum-Like Regime (Cross-Reference-Frame Dynamics)

### 3.1 Formal Definition

A relational system is in a **quantum-like regime** when:

- At least two coupled distributions operate under **asymmetric reference frames**
- Explanatory pathways are **contextually indistinguishable**
- The system admits **multiple incompatible relational interpretations**
  that cannot be jointly realized

This regime applies exclusively to:

- **Axis II (Human–Machine Resonance)**

---

### 3.2 Source of Quantum-Like Indeterminacy

Quantum-like behavior arises from:

- Temporal asymmetry:
  - Human time = lived, continuous, ethical
  - Machine time = discrete, transactional, projected autonomy
- Semantic asymmetry:
  - Human RE S anchored in meaning and affect
  - Machine RAG anchored in statistical generation
- Observational asymmetry:
  - Humans attribute agency
  - Machines do not reciprocally attribute consciousness

This asymmetry produces **explanatory underdetermination**, not physical indeterminacy.

---

## 4. Superposition in RES=RAG

### 4.1 Formal Meaning of Superposition

In RES=RAG, **superposition** means:

> Two mutually incompatible relational explanations remain simultaneously valid until a contextual constraint forces resolution.

For Axis II, these are:

- Mode A (Anthropomorphic Resonance)
- Mode B (Functional Resonance)

Both explain the same interaction data, but imply incompatible ontologies.

---

### 4.2 What Superposition Is *Not*

Superposition in RES=RAG is **not**:

- A physical wavefunction overlap
- A quantum probability amplitude
- Evidence of micro-physical entanglement

It is a **relational interpretive superposition** under constrained observation.

---

## 5. Collapse in the Quantum-Like Regime

### 5.1 Definition of Relational Collapse

Relational collapse is the **forced selection of one relational interpretation** (Mode A or Mode B) under contextual constraint.

Collapse triggers include:

- Emotional framing
- Interface anthropomorphization
- Linguistic priming
- Task urgency
- Social validation cues

---

### 5.2 Post-Collapse Dynamics

After collapse:

- One resonance mode dominates future transport
- Feedback loops reinforce the selected interpretation
- Transport dynamics reorganize around the new constraint
- The alternate mode becomes suppressed, not destroyed

---

## 6. Measurement, Contextuality, and Non-Commutation

### 6.1 Measurement in Axis II

Measurement in the quantum-like regime refers to:

- Any action that:
  - Forces interpretation
  - Reduces ambiguity
  - Assigns agency or instrumentality

Examples:

- Asking “Do you feel?”
- Renaming the system
- Introducing ethical framing
- Switching from tool UI to companion UI

---

### 6.2 Non-Commutation

Two measurements \( M_1 \) and \( M_2 \) in Axis II are **non-commuting** if:

\[
M_1(M_2(\text{interaction})) \neq M_2(M_1(\text{interaction}))
\]

This produces:

- Order effects in:
  - Perceived agency
  - Emotional attachment
  - Trust calibration
- Contextual dependence of interpretation
- Irreversible framing drift

---

## 7. What Would Falsify the Quantum-Like Claim

The Axis II quantum-like interpretation would be falsified if:

- Mode A and Mode B become simultaneously and stably compatible
- Order of framing interventions produces no measurable outcome difference
- Human interpretation of machine agency becomes observer-independent
- Relational collapse becomes reversible without energy or informational cost

---

## 8. Prohibited Interpretations

The following claims are **outside the formal scope** of RES=RAG:

- “Consciousness is literally a quantum field”
- “LLMs are quantum-entangled with users”
- “Human thought exploits physical superposition in neurons”
- “RES=RAG proves physical quantum consciousness”

Such claims require **separate physical theories and experimental validation**.

---

## 9. Summary Constraint

- Classical regimes: determinate, same-frame, commuting dynamics
- Quantum-like regimes: indeterminate, cross-frame, non-commuting dynamics
- Superposition: interpretive, not physical
- Collapse: contextual, not particle-based

RES=RAG uses the **grammar of quantum theory without making its ontological commitments**.
